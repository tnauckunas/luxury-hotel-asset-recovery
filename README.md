Operational Recovery & Inventory-Control Case Study | ~300 Luxury-Hotel Bedding Assets

# Executive Summary

During a large-scale luxury hospitality project, approximately 300 high-value bedding assets required operational recovery following handling, storage and traceability issues.

I was responsible for leading and coordinating the entire recovery operation, establishing a structured workflow covering asset identification, condition triage, cleaning, rewrapping, tagging, storage allocation and location control. My responsibility was to bring the affected inventory back under control, restore traceability and ensure assets could be accurately identified and retrieved.

The recovery programme brought approximately 300 affected assets back under structured operational control while mitigating potential replacement exposure estimated at approximately £350,000.

## The Operational Problem

Approximately 300 luxury-hotel bedding assets had been affected by handling and storage failures, creating problems around condition, identification, traceability and controlled retrieval.

The assets could not simply be treated as normal stored inventory. Each item needed to be assessed, separated according to condition, cleaned where required, rewrapped, correctly identified and returned to a controlled storage location.

Without a structured recovery process, the operation faced several risks:

Loss of visibility over individual assets and their locations.

Difficulty distinguishing recovered, untreated and potentially damaged items.

Inefficient or inaccurate retrieval from storage.

Further deterioration through poor handling or storage.

Unnecessary replacement of assets that could potentially be recovered.

Significant financial exposure, with potential replacement costs estimated at approximately £350,000.

The challenge was therefore not simply moving or storing the assets. It was to establish an end-to-end operational recovery and inventory-control process capable of bringing the entire affected stock back under control.
